# ClauseWatch privacy policy

Last updated 23 October 2026.

What the ClauseWatch browser extension keeps on your device, the two things it can send, and what it never does. The same policy is published at https://guardianvigil.io/clausewatch/privacy/.

## In short

Nothing about you leaves your device. Only two things are ever sent: ClauseWatch downloads signed rule updates from GitHub, and, only if you turn on AI help with your own API key, it sends text to the AI service you chose. There is no account, no tracking and no telemetry.

## What happens on your device

ClauseWatch reads a site's terms and privacy documents when you ask it to: when you click its toolbar button on a page; on a known site, from the pages the known-sites list names; when you paste an address into **Scan a link**; anywhere else, when you right-click a terms link or selected text and choose ClauseWatch; and, if you turn on Automatic mode, when it finds a sign-up form. It fetches those documents from the site itself, without your cookies, and checks them on your device. It never reads what you type into a page.

It keeps on your device only what it needs to work: your settings, the rules you switched off or added yourself, the latest signed rules file it downloaded and when it last checked for one, the sites where you chose "Don't show on this site", and the sites and documents you recently checked (the quotes found, without the documents' full text, for a week; and for the last ten sites, a fingerprint of each document's text so it can tell you when one changed). Removing the extension removes them.

A data-request letter ("Ask them to delete my data") is drafted on your device and sent by your own email program; ClauseWatch sends nothing.

## Rule updates from GitHub

ClauseWatch downloads new releases of its detection rules and known-sites list from the project's public GitHub repository, once a day at most. It uses a release only after checking its signature. The request carries no account, identifier or information about the sites you visit. GitHub sees the request as it would any download, under its own privacy statement.

## If you turn on AI help

AI help is off unless you turn it on. With your browser's built-in model, everything is done on your device and nothing is sent. With your own API key, what you ask about is sent to the AI service you chose, under that service's own terms, including whether it trains on what it receives:

- **Summarise the quotes** sends only the quotes ClauseWatch found;
- **Look for clauses the rules missed** sends the text being checked: the terms or privacy policy you are reading, or the text you pasted or selected, up to its first 24,000 characters.

Your key stays on your device and goes only to that service. Nothing about you or your browsing is added.

## What it never does

Collect, sell or share your personal information. Track your browsing. Send telemetry, analytics or crash reports. Show advertising. Grade, score or rank services.

## Children

ClauseWatch collects no personal information from anyone, children included.
